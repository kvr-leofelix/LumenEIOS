LumenEIOS · Mentor presentation

For the child in the middle

Mentor version · opening about 2 minutes · conversational, for a small room · followed by the meeting guide

Opening (2 minutes)

Sit or stand facing the mentor, not the screen. Speak as if in conversation. Before we show you anything, we'd like to start with a question. [pause]

In your experience, how long does it take a good teacher to know who in a class is going to do well, and who probably won't study at all? [let the mentor answer; agree with whatever they say]

Exactly. Experienced teachers read both ends of a class very quickly. That instinct is real, and we have a lot of respect for it.

Slow down. This is the core idea. But our project started with the children in between. The ones who are trying, who are almost there, who need some extra effort at the right moment. With forty students, a fixed syllabus and lost school days, a teacher simply can't give that extra effort to every one of them. It isn't about caring. It's about time.

And the tools schools have don't help much. A report card gives one number. Sixty in Science doesn't say whether a child is stuck on the wording of a question, the concept, or applying it. Most work is still handwritten, so marking eats up the teacher's evenings. And parents usually find out at the half-yearly exam, which is too late.

The National Education Policy 2020 asks schools to assess understanding, not just marks. LumenEIOS is our attempt to make that practical in an ordinary Indian classroom. It:

  • reads a child's handwritten work and shows where they need help, across four parameters: language, concept, application and critical thinking,
  • gives each child homework that fits and a learning path, based on the five-step Panchapadi model,
  • protects core lessons when school days are lost,
  • and keeps parents and principals informed as it happens.

One principle runs through all of it: the AI only drafts, and the teacher always decides. We are not trying to replace a teacher's instinct. We want to give them an extra pair of eyes for the middle of the class.

Then turn to the screen. We'll show it to you through one student, Gurleen, a Class 7 girl from the middle of her class. And we'd really value your honest feedback on where we can do better.

The research, in four talking points

  1. Competencies, not just marks. NEP 2020 asks for competency-based assessment. We turned that into four parameters a teacher can actually see for every child: Language, Concept, Application and Critical Thinking. Two children with the same marks can need completely different help.
  2. Built on official sources. We studied the current NCERT Class 6–8 Maths and Science books (Ganita Prakash, Curiosity), the NCERT learning-outcomes document and the National Curriculum Framework 2023, using only official NCERT channels. We did not put NCERT content inside the product without permission, and we prepared a permission request.
  3. Indian and classic teaching models. The Panchapadi model (prepare, understand, practise, apply, extend) for the learning path. Bloom's taxonomy for question difficulty. Teach-Back, because explaining an idea is one of the strongest tests of understanding.
  4. Fair to the child. We only suggest extra support after enough teacher-checked work: at least 3 pieces from 2 different activities. Missing evidence is shown as "insufficient evidence", never as zero. A child is never labelled by a lack of data.

Closing for the mentor (after the demo)

So that's LumenEIOS. Gurleen's teacher still knows her class better than any software ever will. What we've tried to do is make sure that the child in the middle doesn't go unnoticed just because there wasn't enough time.

We know there's a lot still to do. If you were in our place, what would you change or focus on first?

Questions to ask the mentor

1. The idea. Does focusing on "the child in the middle" feel right to you, or should we frame the problem differently?
2. Teachers. Would a teacher you know actually use this? What would stop them?
3. A real trial. Could you help us find a school, or a few teachers, to try it with real worksheets?
4. Core and Non-Core. Who should decide which topics are Core: the teacher, the school, or a common standard?
5. Measuring impact. How should we show that it actually helps children? What would convince you?
6. Priorities. We have many features. Which one would you strengthen first, and which could we drop?

If the mentor asks…

QuestionSimple answer
"Isn't this too much for one teacher to learn?"A teacher's daily use is three things: upload or check worksheets, approve homework, and approve the plan. Everything else is optional.
"What makes this different from existing school apps?"Most apps record marks. We show why a child is struggling, protect core learning when days are lost, and keep the teacher in control of every AI decision.
"Why not just use ChatGPT?"Children's work stays at school, every output is checked against the teacher's answer key, and the results connect to the student's record, parents and the school plan.
"How does it work technically?"It's a website with a database of classes and results, plus an AI model that reads handwriting and drafts feedback. We can go deeper if you'd like.

Gurleen, Ms. Garima and all other people, schools and scores in the demo are fictional.
