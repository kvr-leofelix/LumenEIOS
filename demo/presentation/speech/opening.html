LumenEIOS · Opening speech

For the child in the middle

About 1.5 minutes including audience pauses · roughly 230 words · two hand-raises, one question to the room

Walk to the centre. Wait for silence. Raise your own hand as you ask. Quick question. Raise your hand if you ever had a teacher who could tell, just by looking at the class, who was going to top. [wait for hands]

Keep it up if that same teacher also knew who wasn't going to study at all. [wait, smile, hands down]

Teachers are brilliant at this. After years in a classroom, they can spot both ends of the class in a week.

Slow down. Look slowly across the room. But what about the children in between? [pause] The ones who are trying. Who are almost there. Who need a little extra push at the right time. With forty students and one teacher, that push doesn't always come. Not because teachers don't care, but because there aren't enough hours in the day.

Gurleen is one of those children. Class 7, sixty in Science. That number can't tell anyone whether she's stuck on the wording, the idea, or using it in real life. Her teacher is marking forty notebooks at ten at night, and a red alert has just cost the class two days. Her mother will find out at the half-yearly, when it's too late to help.

Ask the room directly. Wait. Be honest. How many of us were a Gurleen once? [let hands go up, then pause]

The National Education Policy asks us to measure understanding, not just marks. So we built LumenEIOS, for the children in the middle. It:

  • reads a child's handwritten page and shows where she needs help,
  • gives every child homework that fits and a learning path from understanding to real life,
  • protects core lessons when school days are lost,
  • and keeps parents and principals informed as it happens, not at term end.

And at every step, the teacher has the final say. We don't replace her instinct. We give her the extra pair of eyes she never had time for.

Pause. Then deliver the last lines clearly. The toppers will be fine.
The child in the middle deserves to be noticed, before it's too late.

Delivery notes

  • Raise your own hand first. People copy what you do. Hold your hand up for two or three seconds so the room has time to join.
  • If few hands go up, smile and say "I'll take that as a yes." Keep going. Never wait more than three seconds.
  • "In between" is the heart of the speech. Stop after it and look across the room before you go on. For a Hindi-speaking crowd you can say this line in Hindi: "Lekin jo beech mein reh jaate hain, unka kya?"
  • The second question ("How many of us were a Gurleen once?") is the emotional turn. Ask it softly and let the silence work. You can raise your own hand here too.
  • Say the four bullet lines steadily, one per breath. Don't speed up.
  • Hand over cleanly. After the last line, turn to the screen and say: "Let us show you how." The demo script begins from there.
  • To bring it down to about one minute, drop the National Education Policy sentence and the sentence "We don't replace her instinct…". Keep both hand-raises and the closing.

Why each line is there

LineWhat it does
The two hand-raisesGets everyone involved in the first ten seconds. Everyone has had this teacher, so the room agrees with you before you've made your point.
"The children in between"The core idea. Teachers already know the toppers and the ones who have given up. LumenEIOS is for the middle, the children who need extra effort a teacher can't always give.
"Wording, idea, or using it in real life"The four competency parameters (Language, Concept, Application, Critical Thinking), without jargon.
"Forty notebooks at ten at night"Handwriting upload, AI-drafted marking and teacher publishing. Also the AI Question Studio.
"A red alert has cost two days"The Time-Aware Engine: lost days recovered, Core lessons protected.
"Her mother will find out at the half-yearly"The student and parent dashboards, with results the same day.
"Homework that fits" and "a learning path"Differentiated Homework and LearnSphere (Panchapadi, Teach-Back, Real-Life Missions).
"How many of us were a Gurleen once?"Turns the problem into something personal. Most of the audience were the child in the middle at some point.
"We don't replace her instinct"Respects teachers' experience and answers the worry about AI replacing them.
