How to use this script

Every step has two columns. The left column lists what is on screen and what to click. The right column is what to say while the audience watches. You don't need to read it word for word. Say it in your own voice, but keep the key sentences in bold.

The story we tell

The opening speech introduced three people: Gurleen, her teacher Ms. Garima, and her mother. The demo shows how LumenEIOS helps each of them, in three acts.

ActProblemWhat we show
1. Lost daysSchool days are lost and teachers are forced to rush.Time-Aware Engine: a lost day is recovered and core learning stays protected.
2. Invisible gapsA total mark hides why a child struggles, and families hear about it too late.A handwritten worksheet is read by AI, checked by the teacher, and reaches the student and parent in minutes.

Before you present (30 minutes earlier)

  1. Restart the app so no earlier AI work is still running: stop-lumeneios, then start-lumeneios. Open http://localhost:3000.
  2. Reset the demo data if you rehearsed with it (this backs up the current data first): .venv/bin/python -m backend.scripts.build_demo_database --fresh, then load the LearnSphere examples with backend.scripts.demo_learnsphere. The second command takes about 15 minutes.
  3. Warm up the AI model: the first AI request after starting loads the model onto the graphics card and is slow. Sign in as Ms. Garima and ask Ask LumenEIOS (bottom-right button) any question, then wait for the answer. Don't use Gurleen's worksheet for this, because uploading it changes the demo.
  4. Keep these ready: the sample sheet demo/answer-sheets/gurleen_7-a_science_worksheet.png, and the backup video demo/presentation/LumenEIOS_showcase.mp4 in case anything fails.
  5. Screen setup: browser zoom 110–125% so the TV is readable. Use the text-size button (A) in the top bar if needed. Close other tabs and turn off notifications.

Act 1 · Lost days · about 6 minutes

When a school day is lost, core learning is never cut

Our flagship feature, the Time-Aware Engine. Teacher plans, principal approves, a day is lost, the plan recovers.

1.1 · Meet the teacher Core · 1 min · sign in as Ms. Garima Sharma
On screen / Do
  1. Sign out, then click the Teacher button (Ms. Garima).
  2. Teacher Dashboard: point at the class summary and "students needing support".
  3. Optional Open My Classes & Heatmap for a few seconds.
Say

This is Ms. Garima. She teaches Science and is the class teacher of 7-A. Like every teacher, she has far more work than hours in the day.

Notice that we don't show one mark per child. NEP 2020 asks schools to look at how a child understands, so every student has four learning parameters:

Language: can they understand the question? Concept: do they know why it works? Application: can they use it in real life? Critical Thinking: can they reason and judge?

Two students can both score 60%, but one may be struggling with the language of the question and the other with the idea itself. They need completely different help.

1.2 · The teacher plans the rest of the year Core · 1.5 min
On screen / Do
  1. Open Time-Aware Engine.
  2. Choose Class VII and Science.
  3. Point at the capacity: days left, periods still to teach, Core and Non-Core. These numbers change with the date, so read them off the screen.
  4. Click Generate draft schedule for approval.
Say

Every teacher in India knows this feeling: it's November, and the question is "Will I finish the syllabus?"

Here the answer is clear. Ms. Garima has about ninety teaching days left. She has taught 43 of 78 topics, and the rest needs around 165 periods.

Here's the key idea. Each topic is marked either Core, which is foundational and must be taught in class by the teacher, or Non-Core, which is enriching and can become guided independent learning if time runs short.

[click Generate] With one click, the engine places every remaining period on the school calendar, all the way to March. It's only a draft. Nothing changes until the principal approves it.

1.3 · The principal approves Core · 45 s · sign in as Dr. Lakshmi Narayanan
On screen / Do
  1. Sign out, then click Principal.
  2. Open Planning Oversight.
  3. Find Draft schedules awaiting approval and click Approve teaching schedule.
Say

The principal, Dr. Lakshmi, sees the draft here. She approves it, and it becomes Ms. Garima's Daily Curriculum, a day-by-day plan for her classroom.

This matters because it means school leadership and teachers share one plan, instead of the principal discovering in February that a class is behind.

1.4 · A red alert closes the school Core · 1.5 min · sign in as Ms. Garima
On screen / Do
  1. Sign in as Ms. Garima again and open Time-Aware Engine.
  2. Scroll to the bottom and click Record a Lost Day.
  3. In Find period, type the lost date (2026-11-27 if you generate the schedule on 20 Nov, see the note below).
  4. Tick both periods on that date and check that both say Core.
  5. Reason: Extreme Weather. Add a short note, then record.
  6. Open the Day tracker tab.
Say

Now real life arrives. [pause] On the 27th of November, a red alert shuts the school. Ms. Garima loses two Science periods, and both of them were Core.

Normally this is where the rushing begins. Instead, she records the lost day. [record]

Straight away the engine proposes a recovery. Both Core periods are still taught in class, in the following days. To make room, some Non-Core work moves to guided independent learning.

The engine follows three promises. Core is never cut. Nothing changes without approval. If something truly cannot fit, it says so openly. It never silently drops it.

Choosing the date: the engine moves Non-Core work out of the next three teaching days to make room. Pick a date where both periods are Core and the next three days contain Non-Core periods; otherwise the engine honestly reports a Core period it could not fit. The schedule is built from the day you click Generate, so the right date changes with that day. Verified on 20 Nov: 26, 27 and 28 Nov, and 5 and 7 Dec, all recover both Core periods; 24 Nov does not. If you present on another day, rehearse once that morning and pick the date then.

A whole school day was lost, and not one Core lesson was lost.

That's Ms. Garima's red alert taken care of. Now, what happens when Gurleen picks up her pen?

From a handwritten page to every dashboard, in minutes

This is the emotional centre of the demo. Slow down here.

On screen / Do

  2. Open Upload. Choose Worksheet: Changes Around Us.
  3. Upload gurleen_7-a_science_worksheet.png.
  4. Click Run OCR. While it reads, talk.
Say

Most classroom work in India still happens on paper. And that's how it should be: children learn by writing. But paper is slow to mark, and the insight stays locked in a notebook.

Gurleen did her Science worksheet by hand. She takes one photo and uploads it. [click Run OCR]

Now an AI model reads her handwriting, word for word. It runs on the school's own computer, so a child's work doesn't need to leave the school.

[when text appears] Here is her answer, typed out exactly as she wrote it, spelling mistakes and all. We don't "correct" a child's words before the teacher has seen them.

If reading takes longer than about 40 seconds, say: "While the model reads, notice that nothing here asks Gurleen for a typed answer. She only needs a phone camera." If it fails, open the backup video or screenshots 35–36.
2.2 · The teacher checks, and decides Core · 2 min · sign in as Ms. Garima
On screen / Do
  1. Sign in as Ms. Garima, open Upload and the same worksheet.
  2. Point at the class list: who has uploaded, who is pending.
  3. Pick Gurleen and click Evaluate with AI.

Say

Ms. Garima opens the same worksheet. At a glance she can see who has handed in and who hasn't. No pile of notebooks on her desk at 10 pm.

She asks the AI for a first draft of the marking. It checks each answer against her answer key. Gurleen gets 5 out of 7, and two answers are flagged as partly correct, with the missing part shown.

[while the AI marks, about 1 to 1.5 minutes] The AI is now reading each answer and comparing it with the teacher's answer key, question by question. Notice it doesn't just look for keywords: it checks whether the reasoning matches.

[when the draft appears] But this is the most important sentence of our whole project: the AI only suggests. The teacher decides. Nothing reaches a student or a parent until Ms. Garima reads it, agrees or changes it, and presses Publish.

We are not replacing the teacher. We're giving her back her evenings, so she can spend her time on the children, not the paperwork. [click Publish]

Act 3 · Closing the gap · about 4 minutes

Seeing a gap is not enough, so we help close it

Sign in once more as Ms. Garima. All of the following is in her sidebar.

3.1 · Differentiated Homework: one child, one need Core · 1.5 min · Ms. Garima
On screen / Do
  1. Open Differentiated Homework.
  2. Choose 7-A, Science, student Mahika Sharma.
  3. Show the gap analysis (weakest: Language).
  4. Click Preview, then show the approve and assign step.
Say

Remember Gurleen getting the same homework as everyone else? In most classrooms, every child does. The child who is already ahead gets bored, and the child who is behind falls further behind.

Meet Mahika, another child from the middle of the class. Her weakest parameter is Language. She often knows the Science, but the wording of the question trips her up. Many children learning in a second language face exactly this.

So the system builds homework for Mahika from the school's own question bank, aimed at her real need. Ms. Garima reviews it and approves it. When it's marked, Mahika's parameters update and her parents are told.

Same class, same teacher, but now every child gets homework that fits.

3.2 · LearnSphere: the Panchapadi path Core · 1.5 min · Ms. Garima
On screen / Do
  1. Open LearnSphere, choose Class 7-A and a student.
  2. Learning Journey: show the five Panchapadi stage graphs.
  3. Teach-Back Challenge: show AI feedback beside the teacher's verified feedback.
  4. Optional Real-Life Missions: Divya Desai's kitchen change hunt or turmeric indicator.
Say

Learning isn't one jump. It's a path. We used Panchapadi, a five-step model of learning from Indian education:

Adheeti, getting ready. Bodh, understanding. Abhyas, practising. Prayog, applying it to life. Prasar, extending it on your own.

The teacher can see which step each child has truly reached. If there isn't enough evidence, we say "insufficient evidence", never a zero. A child is never labelled by a lack of data.

In the Teach-Back Challenge, a child explains a concept in their own words, for example why a sea breeze blows. Because if you can teach it, you understand it. The AI points out what was understood and what was missed, and the teacher confirms it.

And Real-Life Missions take Science into the kitchen, like making a natural indicator from turmeric at home. Learning becomes something a child does, not something they memorise.

Making a good question paper takes hours. Here a teacher uploads a chapter and chooses the level of thinking, from remember up to create, following Bloom's taxonomy, which every B.Ed. teacher learns.

The AI drafts the questions and double-checks every answer key against the chapter. Again, the teacher edits, approves and assigns.

The research behind it, in plain words

Use these if the mentor asks "Where does this come from?"

IdeaWhere it comes from and why we used it
Four parameters (Language, Concept, Application, Critical Thinking)NEP 2020 asks schools to move from rote marks to competency-based assessment. Our four parameters turn that idea into something a teacher can see for every child.
Core and Non-CoreWhen time is short, not every topic carries equal weight. Protecting the foundations first keeps gaps from carrying into the next grade.
NCERT alignmentWe studied the official NCERT Class 6–8 Maths and Science books (Ganita Prakash, Curiosity) and the NCERT learning-outcomes document, using only official NCERT sources. We prepared a permission request to NCERT before using any of their content inside the product.
PanchapadiA five-step Indian learning model: prepare, understand, practise, apply, extend. It describes learning as a path, not a single score.
Bloom's taxonomyThe classic ladder of thinking levels that teachers already use. Our question generator follows it.
Teach-BackExplaining something to someone else is one of the strongest tests of real understanding.
Evidence before judgementWe only recommend support after enough teacher-reviewed work (at least 3 pieces from 2 different activities). Missing data is shown as missing, never as zero.

Likely questions and simple answers

QuestionAnswer
Will AI replace teachers?No. Every AI output is a draft. Only the teacher can publish a mark, approve homework or confirm a learning stage. The AI saves time. It never has the final word.
What if the AI marks wrongly?That is exactly why the teacher reviews it. The system also checks answers against the answer key and flags disagreements so the teacher looks twice.
Is children's data safe?The AI runs on the school's own computer. Parents see only their own child and teachers only their own classes. Every grade and planning change is recorded in an audit trail.
Will it work in rural schools with little technology?Yes, that was our starting point. Students write on paper, and one phone photo is enough. Our demo includes a hill school that started lower and grew faster.
Is the data real?No. All schools, people and scores in the demo are fictional, created so we could show the full journey safely.
What's next?Trying it with a real school, receiving NCERT's permission to use official curriculum content, and adding more subjects and classes.
How does it work technically? (keep it short)It's a website for five kinds of users. Behind it is a database of classes and results, and an AI model that can read handwriting and draft feedback. That's all most audiences need.

If something goes wrong

  • AI is slow: keep talking. Use the "While the model reads…" line in step 2.1. Never stand in silence.
  • AI fails or the screen freezes: say "Let me show you what this looks like", then play LumenEIOS_showcase.mp4 or open the screenshots in docs/screenshots/.
  • Running out of time: skip every Optional step, and cut Act 3 down to Differentiated Homework plus one sentence on Panchapadi. Always keep the closing.
  • Wrong account signed in: sign out and use the one-click button. Calmly say "Let's switch to the teacher's view."
